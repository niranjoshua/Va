The product

A forecast is only useful if you can trust it and act on it.

So Vaticore is built on three commitments: honest uncertainty, honest evaluation, and a decision at the end.

01

Probabilistic by default

Every forecast is a range, not a single number. You see the expected case and the bad case, because a battery sized for the average day fails on the worst one. Ranges are what let you size reserve and decide on backup.

02

Measured against a baseline

Every model is backtested on held-out history and scored against a naive baseline. If it cannot beat "same as before", it does not ship. No cherry-picked numbers, defensible under due diligence.

03

Answers, not charts

The forecast becomes an operating call: the battery reserve to hold and whether to plan backup over the next window, expressed in the terms operators already use. Advisory only. A human always decides.

How it works

From messy meter data to a decision, on a loop.

Step 01

Ingest

Validated schema, timezone normalisation, and explicit gap handling. Built for the sparse, irregular, low-frequency data real operators actually have, not the clean data most models assume.

Step 02

Forecast

Calendar, lag, and weather features drive a probabilistic gradient-boosting model for demand and solar. New sites start from similar sites and sharpen as their own history arrives.

Step 03

Decide

The forecast becomes a battery-reserve and backup advisory with an uncertainty range, refreshed through the day as new readings land. The plan updates; you stay ahead of it.

Evidence

Built to survive due diligence.

  • Probabilistic gradient boosting, evaluated with rolling-origin backtests and scored on pinball loss, the metric that rewards a calibrated range rather than a lucky average.
  • Every run reports the baseline alongside the model. A model that does not beat persistence is reported as such, honestly.
  • Weather-aware. Live and forecast weather feed the demand and solar models, so a cloudy tomorrow shows up in today's plan.
  • Multi-tenant and multi-site from the first commit. One engine, many operators, many sites.

Current figures are from backtests on benchmark and real grid data. Pilot data replaces them, site by site, and the model sharpens the longer it runs.
